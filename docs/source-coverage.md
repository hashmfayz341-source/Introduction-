The lesson follows one cellular story: homeostasis → adaptation or injury →
oxygen/energy failure → ion and metabolic disturbances → interacting damage
mechanisms → recovery or cell death. The first full narration contains 18 scenes,
72 measured speech cues and 1,674 words. Scene duration is determined by real
decoded narration, not by the lecture's slide count or reading-length estimates.

## Prerequisites, priorities and ancillary material

The prerequisite model is intentionally small: a membrane separates compartments,
mitochondria support ATP generation, pumps use ATP, water follows osmotic gradients,
ribosomes synthesize proteins and the nucleus contains DNA. ATP depletion and the
reversible-to-irreversible distinction receive more teaching attention than the
title pages and source dividers. No source slide is treated as an equal-duration scene.

The normal-cell atlas on page 4 also labels cytoplasm, secretory vesicles, Golgi,
peroxisomes, centrioles, nuclear envelope/pores/nucleolus and cytoskeletal components
(microtubules, intermediate filaments and actin). These supply the anatomical context
for material synthesis, transport, breakdown and support; the lecture does not give
each of them a separate injury pathway. The video concentrates specific mechanisms
on the five injury targets explicitly selected on page 14, while this record preserves
the atlas's supporting anatomy instead of inventing extra pathology chapters.

The source's genetic example **“Mediterranean disease”** is retained verbatim as a
source example. It is nonspecific; no claim is made that it uniquely means familial
Mediterranean fever, thalassemia, or any other particular disorder. “Hypoxia most
common” is conveyed as the lecture's emphasis rather than an unsupported universal
ranking for every clinical or experimental context.

## Knowledge and simplification labels

- **Lecture:** the six mechanisms, organelle targets, causative categories, response
  tree, myocyte example and annotated reversible/necrotic morphology.
- **Established clarification:** adaptation definitions; hypoxia versus ischemia;
  limited proliferative capacity of adult permanent cells; preserved functional
  membrane integrity in reversible injury; calcium handling separate from Na/K pumping;
  inner versus outer mitochondrial membranes; ROS includes nonradical H2O2; damage
  repair versus apoptotic execution; brief necrosis/apoptosis morphology comparison.
- **Visual simplification:** not-to-scale organelles, illustrative particle counts,
  schematic gradients/pumps/arrows, compressed interactions, matched diagrams rather
  than tissue microscopy. The order of a reveal does not imply that all mechanisms
  must occur sequentially or in every injured cell.
